Graduations & birthdays

Graduation open houses, the southern Utah way

Graduation in Washington County usually means an open house: the grad in the driveway, a slideshow on the garage door, and the ward, the cousins and the whole team dropping by over a couple of hours. That drop-in rhythm is exactly what a drink trailer is good at. Guests walk up, order a drink their way, and stay to talk instead of grabbing a can and leaving. We do open houses for graduates from Desert Hills, Dixie, Pine View, Snow Canyon, Crimson Cliffs, Hurricane and the other high schools across the county, plus college grads from Utah Tech and Dixie Technical College.

We’ll name a drink after the graduate and match the syrups to the school colors, and keep the menu short so the line moves when the crowd arrives all at once. Graduation open houses tend to land on the same few weekends, so book as early as you can.

Birthdays for every age

Kids’ birthdays are the easy ones: Beach Babies kid sizes, soft-serve cones and anything blue. Sweet sixteens and eighteenths get the full board and a drink named for the birthday. Then there are the milestone birthdays: a fiftieth with the neighbors, an eightieth with four generations in the backyard, a surprise party at a rental house. The grandparents can have a caffeine-free lemonade or a Diet Coke with coconut, and the toddlers get a cone.

Family milestones

The parties St. George families throw don’t stop at birthdays. Baptism-day lunches, baby showers, anniversaries, retirement parties, family reunions, and the missionary farewells and homecomings that bring the whole family to one backyard (see church and school events). Bridal showers and receptions have their own page: southern Utah weddings.

Where the trailer parks

Most of these parties happen at home, and that is the easiest setup. We need a flat spot that a truck and trailer can pull into, like a driveway, a side yard or the curb out front, close enough to your guests that the line feels like part of the party. The trailer runs on its own generator and carries its own water. If your neighborhood has an HOA, check its rules on vendors and street parking. A party that spills into a public street or uses a city park beyond a normal reservation can need a St. George special event permit, and the city asks for those applications at least 45 days ahead; park reservations go through City Parks. Washington City, Santa Clara, Ivins and Hurricane have their own rules. More on HOA and neighborhood parties.

How the tab works

Most families cover everyone. You can also set a cap, cover the first drink, or let guests buy their own after that. Tell us the head count and how long the open house runs, and we’ll suggest the right package and a menu that fits your crowd. More about the trailer on dirty soda in St. George.

Open houses in the heat

Graduation and summer birthdays land in the hottest part of the year, when afternoons regularly pass 100 degrees. Evening open houses work best, after the sun drops. Every drink is poured over nugget ice, and a soft-serve float is hard to beat on a hot night. For a midday party we’ll suggest a short menu and a shaded spot for the line.

Questions

Before you book

Can you come to a graduation open house?

Yes. Open houses are one of the parties we built the trailer for. Send the date, the address area, the head count and how long the open house runs, and we’ll reply with a price within one business day.

How far ahead should we book for graduation?

As early as you can, since graduation open houses tend to land on the same few weekends. For a birthday or a weeknight party, ask anyway: if the trailer is free, we’ll come.

Do you have kid sizes for a kids’ birthday?

Yes. The Beach Babies menu is sized and priced for kids, and soft-serve cones are always a hit. Caffeine-free drinks are easy too.

Can you set up at a city park?

Often, with a park reservation, and the city’s rules come first. Anything beyond a normal reservation can need a St. George special event permit, applied for at least 45 days ahead. A home is the simplest setup.

Can guests pay for their own drinks?

Yes. Most families cover everyone, but you can set a cap or cover the first drink and let guests buy their own after that.

Can you name a drink after the graduate?

Yes. We’ll build a signature drink, match it to the school colors and put the graduate’s name on it for the day.
