Southern Utah weddings

A St. George reception, done the Utah way

A lot of St. George weddings follow the same rhythm: a sealing or ceremony earlier in the day, a family luncheon, then an evening reception or open house where half the neighborhood comes through the line. Most of those receptions are dry, so the drink bar is the bar. In the town where Utah’s best-known chain started, dirty soda is the obvious pick, and a trailer that pours to order for every guest turns the drink line into part of the party instead of a table of warm cans.

Guests line up for a Thirst Trap (Dr Pepper, coconut, sweet cream) or a Mountain Dew with mango and watermelon. The kids get soft-serve cones, the grandparents get a caffeine-free lemonade, and the photographer gets a neon-pink trailer against a red-rock sky.

Built for receptions in the heat

Southern Utah’s wedding season runs long. Spring and fall evenings are close to perfect, and summer receptions simply start later, after the sun drops behind the ridge. Every drink is poured over nugget ice, and the soft-serve machine turns any soda into a float, which is hard to beat at a July reception. For daytime summer events we’ll suggest a shorter menu and a time window that keeps the line moving, so nobody in a suit waits long on hot pavement.

Make it yours

We’ll name a signature drink after the two of you and match the syrups and toppings to your colors, so sage and terracotta or navy and gold both work. Keep the menu to five or six drinks so the rush after the cake moves fast, or open the full board for a long open house. Add espresso for a late dance floor, or sweet-cream cold brew for a morning luncheon.

Backyards, venues and where the trailer parks

We do backyard receptions in Little Valley, Bloomington and Green Valley, family-property open houses in Washington City and Santa Clara, red-rock patios out in Ivins and Kayenta, and receptions out toward Hurricane and the road to Zion. The trailer needs a flat spot that a truck and trailer can pull into, close enough to your guests that the line feels like part of the party. It runs on its own generator and carries its own water.

One thing to check first: some resort, golf and event venues require all food and drinks to come from their own kitchen. Look at your venue contract or ask your coordinator whether outside food and beverage vendors are allowed. If they are, we’ll work with the coordinator on load-in time and parking before we quote. If your reception is in Ivins, Santa Clara or Hurricane, those pages cover the area, and dirty soda in St. George covers the rest of the county.

Showers, luncheons, ring ceremonies and open houses

Bridal showers, ring ceremonies, sealing-day luncheons and backyard open houses can use the smaller Sip & Go package: the same trailer, a shorter list and a smaller bill. Missionary-age siblings, cousins from the Wasatch Front and the grandkids all end up at the window, so we always bring kid sizes and zero-sugar versions.

Questions

Before you book

Can you name a drink after us?

Yes. Tell us what the two of you love, and we’ll build a signature drink, match it to your colors and put your names on it for the night.

How many guests can you serve at a big St. George reception?

Every drink is built to order, so for a reception of a few hundred guests we’ll recommend a short menu and the right number of hours. Put your head count in the form and we’ll plan around it.

Can you serve at our venue in Ivins, Kayenta or Hurricane?

If the venue allows outside food and beverage vendors, yes. Some resort and event venues require everything from their own kitchen, so check your contract first. Backyards and family property are the easiest.

What about a summer reception?

Start after the heat breaks if you can. We pour over nugget ice, soft-serve floats are the favorite, and we’ll suggest a menu and a time window that keep the line short.

Can guests pay for their own drinks?

Most couples cover everyone, but you can also set a cap and switch to guests paying after that. Tell us which you want in the form.

How far ahead should we book?

For a Saturday reception, the sooner the better. A month or more out is ideal. For a shower or a weeknight open house, ask anyway: if the trailer is free, we’ll come.
