## LaVerkin: canals, orchards and the overlook

LaVerkin sits between Hurricane and Toquerville, across the river bridge from Hurricane. It was farmland first, then a surveyed townsite, and the LaVerkin Canal, the LaVerkin Fruit and Nursery Company, the old hot springs at Pah Tempe and the LaVerkin Overlook, with its view across the Hurricane Valley, are all part of the town's story. It is still a small town, and a backyard there is an easy place to park a drink trailer.

## Toquerville: the old town on Ash Creek

Toquerville is about nineteen miles northeast of St. George on State Route 17. Its settlers built near the village of Toquer, the Paiute leader who befriended them, and the town took his name; the Washington County Historical Society says the word means black, for the volcanic mountain the town sits beneath. Toquerville had the first cotton gin in the state, grew fruit in its warm climate and was even a county seat for a while. Today it is a quiet town where a graduation open house or a reunion fills the whole street, and that is the kind of party we built the trailer for.

## Leeds and Silver Reef

Leeds sits along I-15 near exits 22 and 23. It was organized as Bennington and renamed after Leeds, England, where its bishop had served a mission. Its first business was grapes and wine, and then it supplied the nearby mining town of Silver Reef. Leeds passed its own historic preservation ordinance, and the Silver Reef Museum now fills the old Wells Fargo Express building, on a monument site owned by Washington County. Historic sites and museums set their own rules, so an event there goes through them first. A backyard in Leeds is simpler.

## A trailer that brings its own power

Out here the party is often on a big lot, a family property or a ranch-style yard without an outlet anywhere near it. That is fine: the trailer runs on its own generator and carries its own water, so we only need a flat spot that a truck and trailer can pull into. We do backyard receptions, reunions, birthday parties, missionary farewells and homecomings, ward activities and booster nights. These towns are a longer drive from St. George, so we'll include any travel in the quote, and the math works best when there's a crowd.
