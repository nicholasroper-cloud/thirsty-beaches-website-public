Company parties

The office soda run, for the whole team at once

In St. George the afternoon soda run is practically a workplace tradition: somebody takes a list, drives to a drive-thru and comes back with a tray of drinks that are already watering down. A drink trailer does it the other way around. We park in your lot, everyone walks out and orders their own, built to order over nugget ice, and the whole team takes a break together. In the town where Utah’s best-known chain started, a dirty soda is the thank-you people actually want.

We add the two things a drive-thru can’t: real soft serve, so any soda can become a float, and espresso pulled to order for iced lattes and sweet-cream cold brew. Zero-sugar sodas, caffeine-free lemonades and Italian sodas cover the people who are counting.

Built for the way St. George works

A lot of St. George doesn’t work nine to five. Hospital and clinic teams run around the clock, the school district and the university have their own calendars, an airline is headquartered here, and tech companies have moved into Tech Ridge, on the site of the old municipal airport. Add dealerships, builders and construction crews, resorts, hotels and shops. Every one of them has an employee appreciation week, a goal that got hit, or a summer party that needs something better than a sheet cake.

Shift changes and night crews

For teams that work around the clock, one visit can catch two shifts. We can open early with espresso and cold brew for the crew coming off nights, then pour sodas and floats for the day shift, or split the visit into two windows on the same day so nobody gets left out. Tell us your shift times in the form and we’ll plan around them. Hospitals, plants and other secure sites usually have their own rules about where a vendor can park; we’ll follow whatever your facilities team sets.

Customer appreciation and open houses

Grand openings, model-home weekends, dealership sales events, client open houses and customer appreciation days all draw a crowd that lingers if there’s something cold in hand. The company can cover every drink, cover the first round, or let guests buy their own. You’ll need the property owner’s OK for the spot. A party that spills into a public street or uses a city park beyond a normal reservation can need a St. George special event permit, and the city asks for those applications at least 45 days ahead; park reservations go through City Parks. Washington City, Santa Clara, Ivins and Hurricane have their own rules.

Conventions, expos and team weekends

Conferences, expos and company retreats come with rules of their own. Convention centers and resorts often run their own food and drinks, so we check the venue’s rules first; if outside vendors are allowed, we’ll work with the event team on load-in and parking. A booth crew, a team tent or an after-party at a rental house nearby is usually simpler.

Questions

Before you book

Can the company cover everyone’s drinks?

Yes. Cover the whole team, set a cap, or switch to guests paying once the tab runs out. Tell us which you want in the form.

Can you visit twice in one day for different shifts?

Often, yes. Put your shift times in the notes and we’ll quote one longer window or two shorter ones, so the night crew and the day crew both get a drink.

Can you send an invoice?

Mention it in the notes and we’ll set up billing your company can work with, including a purchase order if you need one, when we send the quote.

Do you do morning events?

Yes. We pull espresso to order, so iced lattes and sweet-cream cold brew are ready for a morning all-hands, an open house or a client breakfast.

What do you need at our building?

A flat spot a truck and trailer can pull into, close to the door, and the property owner’s OK. The trailer runs on its own generator and carries its own water. If your facility has vendor rules, tell us and we’ll follow them.

Can we name a drink after our company?

Yes. We’ll build a custom drink in your colors and name it for your company, product or team.
