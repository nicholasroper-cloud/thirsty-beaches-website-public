## St. George has soda shops. We're the one that drives to you.

Nobody in St. George needs dirty soda explained. One of Utah's best-known soda chains opened its very first shop here in 2010, and the drive-thru soda line has been part of town life ever since. We're not trying to replace your usual stop. What a drive-thru can't do is roll up to your reception, your ward party or your office parking lot and pour for everyone at once. That's the whole idea of the trailer. Our Thirst Trap is the classic (Dr Pepper, coconut, sweet cream), and from there the board runs to 114 drinks, or you can [build your own](https://thirstybeaches.com/#build) and show us the screen at the window.

## Built for southern Utah heat

St. George gets an early start on summer. Spring shows up while the Wasatch Front is still shoveling, the 90s arrive by May, and July and August run past 100. That's float weather for half the year, and it's what the trailer is built for: every drink over nugget ice, and a real soft-serve machine so any soda can turn into a float, with cones, cups and shakes for anyone skipping the soda. We pull espresso to order too, for iced lattes and sweet-cream cold brew at a 7 a.m. event before the heat sets in. Zero-sugar sodas, caffeine-free lemonades and Italian sodas, and Beach Babies kid sizes cover the whole family.

## St. George, neighborhood by neighborhood

St. George is a lot of small places stitched together, and each has its own kind of party. Down by the Southern Parkway, [Desert Color](https://thirstybeaches.com/dirty-soda-desert-color) has a white-sand lagoon, vacation rentals and a community calendar with food-truck nights, so it's rental-house reunions and pool-day birthdays. On the southwest side, [Bloomington and Green Valley](https://thirstybeaches.com/dirty-soda-bloomington-green-valley) go back to a pioneer settlement on the Virgin River and a ranch that became a resort neighborhood, and the parties are in big backyards and at HOA clubhouses. Out east, Little Valley is tournament country (see [tournaments and team parties](https://thirstybeaches.com/st-george-tournaments-team-parties)), and the 55+ communities like SunRiver run clubhouse socials of their own (see [HOA and neighborhood parties](https://thirstybeaches.com/st-george-hoa-neighborhood-party)). Wherever you are in town, the trailer comes to you.

## Dixie Center, Town Square and the big weekends

St. George is a town of big weekends: expos and conventions at the Dixie Center, the St. George Art Festival on Town Square over Easter weekend, marathon and triathlon weekends, Swiss Days in Santa Clara and Peach Days in Hurricane. Running a booth, a vendor row, a team tent or an after-party at one of them? Ask about a pop-up where guests buy their own drinks, or a covered tab for your crew. Venues and city events set their own vendor rules, so we'll check what yours needs before we quote, and we'll only list a public stop once it's actually booked.

## The small towns around St. George

The trailer covers all of Washington County, not just St. George proper, and the towns around it have pages of their own. [Washington City](https://thirstybeaches.com/dirty-soda-washington-city) and Coral Canyon are next door. [Santa Clara](https://thirstybeaches.com/dirty-soda-santa-clara) is the old Swiss pioneer town of Swiss Days and Santa Clara Drive, and [Ivins](https://thirstybeaches.com/dirty-soda-ivins) has Kayenta and the red-rock resorts by Snow Canyon. [Hurricane](https://thirstybeaches.com/dirty-soda-hurricane-sand-hollow) has Sand Hollow's red-sand beach, and north of it [LaVerkin, Toquerville and Leeds](https://thirstybeaches.com/dirty-soda-laverkin-toquerville-leeds) are small towns with a lot of history, from the Ash Creek settlement to the Silver Reef museum. Further out, like Winchester Hills, Dammeron Valley, Veyo, Cedar City or Springdale, just ask and we'll put any travel in the quote.
