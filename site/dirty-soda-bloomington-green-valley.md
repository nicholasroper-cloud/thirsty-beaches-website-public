## Bloomington: the town before the town

Bloomington was a pioneer settlement before it was a St. George neighborhood. The Carpenter family settled the northwest side of the Virgin River and called the place Jamestown; they raised cotton and sugar cane, made molasses and started a broom business. The surveyor renamed it Bloomington, and by the middle of the last century the families were gone. Then a developer began marketing a country club community here, with a golf course at its heart. The Washington County Historical Society calls it the first planned community in Utah and the spark that finally put St. George on the map. Today Bloomington is golf-course streets, the old Manzanita Road settlement and Bloomington Petroglyph Park, all on the river side of town.

## Green Valley: a ranch that became a resort

Green Valley, on the west side of town, started as a six-hundred-acre ranch. Once it was annexed into St. George, the first subdivision went in, and then The Park at Green Valley, which came with an owners association, a clubhouse, a pool and courts at a time when that was new for St. George. The Sports Village, Las Palmas, The Cottages and the Green Valley Spa followed, and condos that were rented to visitors helped make Green Valley a place people come to stay. That mix of long-time owners and visiting families is exactly who books a trailer: a clubhouse social for the residents, or a reunion for the family that rented three condos for the week.

## Backyards, cul-de-sacs and clubhouse lots

The parties we hear about on the southwest side are mostly at home: a birthday in a big Bloomington backyard, a graduation open house in the driveway, a missionary homecoming on the cul-de-sac, a pool-day social at the HOA clubhouse. ## Golf-course neighborhoods and club rules

Country clubs and resorts usually run their own food and drinks, and their event contracts often say so. If your event is at a clubhouse or a resort, check with its events team first; if outside vendors are allowed, we'll work with them on where to park and when to load in. A party at a home on the course is simpler: you decide, as long as your HOA agrees.

## Early tee times and hot afternoons

Southwest St. George mornings start early. We pull espresso to order, so a 7 a.m. group gets iced lattes and sweet-cream cold brew, then the board switches to dirty sodas, lemonades and floats over nugget ice as the day warms up. Summer afternoons regularly pass 100 degrees, so for a midday summer party we'll suggest a short menu and a morning or evening window. Zero-sugar sodas, caffeine-free lemonades and Italian sodas, and Beach Babies kid sizes cover the whole family.

**Can you serve at a country club or resort?**

If the venue allows outside food and beverage vendors, yes. Many clubs and resorts run their own food and drinks, so check your event contract or ask the events team first.

**Can our HOA book you at the clubhouse?**

Yes, with the HOA's OK. The HOA can cover everyone, set a cap, or let residents buy their own. Tell us what the HOA requires from vendors and we'll confirm we can meet it before we quote.

**Do you need power or water at the house?**

No. The trailer runs on its own generator and carries its own water. We just need a flat spot a truck and trailer can pull into.
