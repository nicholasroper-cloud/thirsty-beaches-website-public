## A beach inside St. George

Desert Color is the master-planned community along I-15 and the Southern Parkway, on the land east of the interstate. Its centerpiece is the lagoon at The Shores: about two and a half acres of water with more than half a mile of white-sand shoreline, which the community calls the largest private body of water in the St. George area. People swim, kayak and paddleboard there in a desert town. We named the trailer Thirsty Beaches and built it around one idea, bringing the beach to you, so a neighborhood with its own beach is about as close to home as it gets. Utah's best-known chain started in St. George, so nobody here needs dirty soda explained. What we add is real soft serve and fresh espresso in the same trailer, so any soda can become a float and the morning crowd gets an iced latte.

## Rental houses, reunions and pool-day parties

Desert Color has vacation rentals, some with a private pool, and families book them so three generations can stay under one roof for the week. That is a perfect private booking for us: we pull up to the house for a couple of hours, pour for everybody, and the cousins line up for floats. Check your rental's rules on vendors and parking first, and make sure a truck and trailer can pull onto a flat spot nearby. Residents throw the same parties at home: a birthday by the pool, a graduation open house, a welcome party for the family that just moved onto the street. Kid sizes, zero-sugar sodas and caffeine-free lemonades and Italian sodas mean the grandparents and the toddlers both get something.

## The resort side runs its own drinks

The Shores Resort has its own pool-deck café, Atara has its own restaurants, and Crush Golf + Grill has its own dining and private event spaces. Those places run their own food and drinks, so we don't set up at the lagoon, the resort pools or a restaurant without their invitation, and an event held there goes through them first. The community also sets the rules for its pools, parks and amenities. For a lagoon day, the easy version is to meet you back at your home or rental afterward, when everyone is sandy, sunburned and thirsty.

## A neighborhood calendar that never stops

Desert Color runs a busy community calendar of its own. It lists summer kickoff events with food trucks, dive-in movie nights, holiday lights, car shows, pinewood derbies and moonlight 5Ks, and it has its own parks, about six miles of trails and a pickleball park at Akoya Pearl Community Park with six courts, a performing pavilion and an activity lawn. If you sit on an HOA, a neighborhood committee or an event team and want a drink trailer at a community night, talk to whoever runs the event and then to us. Organizers set their own vendor rules, so we'll check what yours needs before we quote. The HOA can cover everyone, set a cap, or let neighbors buy their own. ## Floats on the hottest days

Summer afternoons in St. George regularly pass 100 degrees, and that is exactly when a soft-serve float beats everything else on the board. Every drink is poured over nugget ice, and cones and shakes cover anyone skipping the soda. For a midday summer party we'll suggest a short menu and a morning or evening window so nobody waits in the sun. Morning crowds headed to pickleball get espresso and sweet-cream cold brew; evening crowds get the full dirty soda board.
